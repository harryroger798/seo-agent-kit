# Evidence standard

## The tiers

| Tier | Source | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Google documentation, Search Console help, status dashboard, official statements | Definitional. It tells you what the system says it does. |
| **B** | Large independent studies with published method | Strong directional evidence. Check sample, date, and vertical. |
| **C** | Vendor research, expert surveys, correlations | Hypotheses and benchmarks. Never quote as law. |
| **D** | Your own audit, client result, practitioner observation | Fastest to act on, weakest to generalise. |

## The rules

1. No source, no claim.
2. Date every number.
3. Name the sample.
4. Separate correlation from causation.
5. Label vendor research.
6. Never invent experience.
7. Never fake reviews, prices, or scarcity.
8. Quote the primary source.
9. Update the page when the fact changes.
10. Show the correction path.

## How to cite in a deliverable

```text
In our 4 November 2026 audit of eight sites, five had no valid sitemap
or a soft-404 sitemap (first-party audit, tier D).
```

```text
AI Overviews reduced the top-ranking page's CTR by 58% in a 300,000-keyword
study using December 2025 data (Ahrefs, 4 February 2026, tier B).
```

## What not to write

- “Studies show…” without a study.
- “Experts agree…” without a name.
- “Everyone knows…” — nobody knows.
- “Our proprietary AI…” — a model is not a source.
- “Up to 10x…” — up to is not a measurement.

## The one question

> If a customer who knows this subject read this page, would they trust us more or less?

If the answer is less, the problem is not the prompt. It is the proof.
