# Evidence rules

These rules apply to every prompt, skill, and pipeline in the kit.

1. **No source, no claim.** If you cannot link to it in ten seconds, it does not go in the draft.
2. **Date every number.** A 2024 statistic is not a 2026 fact.
3. **Name the sample.** “A study” is not a source; “8,500 keywords, May 2026” is.
4. **Separate correlation from causation.** “Associated with” is not “causes.”
5. **Label vendor research.** A tool’s study is useful; it is not neutral.
6. **Never invent experience.** Use a real case or a labelled illustration.
7. **Never fake reviews, prices, or scarcity.** It is a trust and legal risk.
8. **Quote the primary source.** Link to Google, the standards body, or the original paper.
9. **Update the page when the fact changes.** Stale facts are worse than no facts.
10. **Show the correction path.** A page without a way to report an error is not trustworthy.

## Tiers

| Tier | Source | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| A | Google docs, Search Console help, status dashboard | Definitional |
| B | Large independent studies with method | Strong directional |
| C | Vendor research, surveys, correlations | Hypothesis and benchmark |
| D | Your own audit or client result | Fastest to act on, weakest to generalise |

## The one question

> If a customer who knows this subject read this page, would they trust us more or less?

If the answer is less, the problem is not the prompt. It is the proof.
